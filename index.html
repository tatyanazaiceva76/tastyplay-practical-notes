UK to US crochet terms: what dc means, and when to stop

Quick referenceCheck a term listKnow the limits

In UK crochet terminology, dc corresponds to US sc. In a US pattern, dc corresponds to UK tr. The letters alone are not enough: check which convention the pattern uses before choosing a stitch.

A conversion table is useful after that decision, not instead of it. Start with the pattern's own introduction and abbreviation key. If the convention is unstated, ask the designer or publisher rather than guessing from the website address, spelling or the author's location.

Check a term list without rewriting a pattern

For a short list, the UK/US crochet-term audit lets you choose the direction explicitly and check one exact abbreviation per line. It reports supported counterparts and leaves unknown entries visible. It does not decide whether your pattern is British or American.

  1. Confirm the pattern's convention from its own documentation.
  2. Select UK terms to US counterparts, or the reverse if your source is US.
  3. Enter the abbreviations you want to check, one per line, with the exact lowercase spelling.
  4. Read every row. Return unknown terms to the pattern key instead of treating them as translated instructions.

For example, an explicitly UK list containing dc, htr and a designer's custom label should produce two supported counterparts and one item that still needs the designer's definition. That last item is a useful stop signal, not permission to invent a stitch.

Why a whole instruction can remain unknown

The audit checks exact tokens, not sentences. 3 dc is a stitch count plus a term; dc2tog is a different abbreviation. Neither is automatically reduced to dc. Case, spaces and plurals also matter. A line that remains unknown is not proof that the original pattern is wrong.

The tool's eight-pair scope is deliberately small. It does not interpret charts, check stitch totals, calculate repeats or validate turning-chain instructions. Copying a complete pattern into the box will not turn it into a reliable translated pattern.

Before starting the project

Keep the original pattern beside your checked term list. Resolve custom definitions before working those steps, and use the pattern's own instructions for counts, repeats and construction. A terminology counterpart does not confirm the rest of the project instructions.
